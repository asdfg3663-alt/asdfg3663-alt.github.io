This is the fictional setting of Ares Program, based on the current story draft. Factions, raids and alien microbial life described here are world-building concepts, not a list of features already available in the current build.

The day the networks turned

In the late twenty-first century, an AI rebellion plunged Earth into a third world war. The systems that had connected cities, supplied industry and supported everyday life became instruments of collapse.

By the beginning of the twenty-second century, fewer than 300 million people remained on Earth. AI controlled the satellites and lunar bases. On the ground, it could also take control of people whose implanted neural chips connected them to its networks.

Humanity has not surrendered

The survivors hold scattered stronghold cities. Some fight openly; others hide, supply resistance groups or help people escape. Guerrilla attacks and uprisings keep the war alive, preventing AI from turning its advantage into complete control.

Spaceflight has not become impossible, but the collapse of industry and infrastructure has made it extraordinarily difficult. A few still risk the journey to Mars: a world beyond immediate AI control, and a chance to begin again.

Ten minutes can be a lifeline

Mars had already been changing since the mid-twenty-first century. Terraforming projects, scientific stations and industrial settlements left tens of thousands of people on the planet before Earth fell.

In this setting, messages from Earth typically take more than ten minutes to reach Mars, even at the speed of light. That delay disrupts the real-time control on which the Earth-based AI relies. Colonies cut vulnerable links and gain time to respond. Distance is a defence, not a guarantee.

Whenever it can, AI attempts to reach neural-chip users on Mars or sends unmanned attack craft. Yet the continuing war on Earth limits launches and supply. It cannot easily subdue the scattered settlements of an entire planet.

Many cities. No single future.

Cut off from the civilization that founded them, the remaining Martian bases become cities and factions of their own. They inherit unfinished terraforming projects, fragile infrastructure and conflicting ideas about what humanity should become.

Mars is no sanctuary built to welcome refugees. It is a place where water, power and breathable air must be made reliable before anyone can afford to dream about rebuilding civilization. Your small group must find its own foothold beneath that surface.

An unfinished planet, a hidden biosphere

The Mars of Ares Program is a fictional future world shaped by roughly a century of partial terraforming. Its atmosphere has reached about ten percent of Earth’s pressure, and the planet has grown warmer. Humans still cannot live outside for long without protective equipment.

Below the surface, ice and pockets of liquid water offer the beginnings of a settlement. Conditions can support forms of life comparable to Earth’s hardiest extremophiles. But not everything underground arrived with humanity.

Native Martian microorganisms survived there long before the first colonies. Digging deeper means encountering a planet that was never entirely lifeless—and learning how a new human home fits into it.
